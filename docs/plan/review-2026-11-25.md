# Review of 25/11/2026 and improvement plan

**What this is.** David asked why "nothing is ever good" (no confirmed analog site on the Moon or Mars), and whether the scenarios are coherent between the two bodies. Five AI reviewers, each given an expert role, read the project and searched the web:
- a lunar geologist;
- a Mars analog scientist;
- an instrument test engineer;
- an analog campaign manager;
- a market and methods analyst.

**These are not real experts.** Their reviews prepare the questions for real ones (phase E). Detail and sources: [wiki/review/expert-panel-2026-11-25](../wiki/review/expert-panel-2026-11-25.md).

## Answer to David's question
1. **The selection criterion causes the failures, not the absence of analogs.** We rank on one property: slope at 1 to 11.6 km. Real analog sites are chosen for their function: what must be imitated for a given use (rock, minerals, ice, impact process, access). At kilometre scale, "resembles Mars" means "flat", and "resembles the lunar shadowed craters" means "hilly", even under a forest.
2. **Our controls are tested on the wrong dimension.** The Hawaiian and Idaho sites (R29) are analogs for their basalt. Their geology matches at all 4 sites, but the frozen rule tests them on slope, where they fail. The same holds for the lunar controls.
3. **Moon and Mars are not coherent.** The Moon has use cases (base, robotics, instruments); Mars has only terrains. The data model already gives Martian targets to the instruments (scenario C), but the interface does not show them. The Moon "Instrument calibration" mission also still carries the v2 text ("dry, calm, cold", seismometer), which K2 rejected.
4. **What everyone keeps:**
   - honesty (unfavourable results first, missing data never a bad value);
   - every land cell assessed;
   - comparison at the planetary pixel;
   - frozen protocols;
   - provenance and hashes.

   No other global, reproducible tool covering both bodies was found.

## Proposed direction: use-case presets, common to the Moon and Mars
**A preset is keyed on the test objective**, for example:
- rover mobility;
- crew geology training;
- radar or GPR;
- spectroscopy;
- drilling and ice;
- ISRU;
- navigation;
- thermal.

**For each body, a preset chooses:**
- the targets;
- the **primary dimension** it compares: slope for mobility, rock for geology, ice for drilling;
- its **gates**: requirements stated as met, failed or unknown;
- the context shown;
- the scale it needs;
- **controls matched to its dimension**;
- its outputs.

**What a preset never does:**
- re-weight similarity (no weighted sum: doctrine kept);
- reuse a lunar threshold on Mars.

The four mission cards of each body become presets. The interface, the API and the validation follow them.
